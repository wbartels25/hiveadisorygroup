Common questions

Common questions about AI strategy and roadmaps

What is an AI strategy and roadmap?

An AI strategy and roadmap is a prioritized, sequenced plan for which AI investments to make, in what order, and what business outcome each should deliver. Hive builds roadmaps over a 12 to 36 month horizon, tied directly to revenue and margin rather than a generic framework. Each one is sequenced around the organization's competitive position, with the aim of identifying where to win rather than presenting a menu of options.

What time horizon should an AI roadmap cover?

The AI roadmaps we build cover a 12 to 36 month horizon, moving from short-term alignment to long-term scaled capability. Strategic alignment takes place in the first four weeks, followed by opportunity mapping in months 2 to 3 and architecture and sequencing in months 3 to 6. First deployments with measurable returns come in months 6 to 12, and scaling that capability across the enterprise runs from months 12 to 36.

How is an AI roadmap tied to revenue and margin?

Each initiative in our AI roadmaps is mapped to a measurable business outcome linked to revenue or margin. Every investment carries a thesis connecting it to competitive advantage, and we avoid vanity metrics that do not reflect financial results. Use cases are ranked by impact, feasibility and time-to-value across business units, which gives leadership a clear basis for deciding which initiatives to fund and in what order.

Do we need an AI readiness assessment before building an AI roadmap?

We would usually suggest completing a readiness assessment first, because it documents the constraints a roadmap has to be sequenced against. In our program, the AI Readiness Assessment is Phase 01 and Strategy & Roadmap is Phase 02. The assessment closes with a 90-day action plan and an optional Phase 2 roadmap alignment session, so the strategy work starts from an evidence-based view of data, talent and infrastructure.

How does an AI roadmap account for budget, talent and data constraints?

An AI roadmap that can actually be executed is sequenced against the organization's real constraints, including budget, talent, data maturity and appetite for change. In our roadmaps, technology stack decisions, integration architecture and phased sequencing are worked through in months 3 to 6, before first deployments. What tends to work is ordering initiatives so early deployments deliver measurable returns and build organizational confidence ahead of larger investments.
